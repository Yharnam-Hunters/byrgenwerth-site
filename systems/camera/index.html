Camera

The follow camera: how it turns the stick, the character's movement and a lock-on target into a camera position each frame, how it keeps out of walls, and where its field of view comes from.

At a glance

Scope
Camera update, targeting and lock-on, collision with the world, field of view.
Verified
The whole follow-camera update, camera_update_follow (20 KB), and the pitch and yaw getters are replaced and edge-verified, each with an independent review.
Our option
BB_TARGET_FPS (30, 60, uncapped) reproduces what the frame-rate patches change in the update.
Open
Who calls the update and sets its requests, the parameter table's other fields, and what the camera looks like in the running game.

Summary

What the follow camera does each frame

camera_update_follow (0x0183ac60) takes the camera, the character it follows (the subject), a world to cast spheres against, and the frame's time step. It works in stages:

  1. Parameters. It picks a row from the lock-camera parameter table and eases the camera toward it: distance, lowest pitch, lock-on pitch shift, height above the subject and vertical field of view. The row's key is the map's ID, replaced in turn by an event ID, a boss ID while a boss lock is requested, and an explicit override, whichever are set; failing that, key 0. It also sets rotation speeds from an option rate (squared, between a minimum and a maximum). With no subject the update stops here.
  2. Carry-along. It moves last frame's camera and subject points by a stored velocity and turns them by a stored angular velocity around a pivot that it reaches through the subject.
  3. Stick. It reads a stick vector from the subject, or a default one. Stick input marks the camera as hand-controlled. Once the stick is released, a wait and then a release time hand control back.
  4. Target. It chases the subject's position and places the target point at the subject plus the parameter row's offset. The camera's pivot (fulcrum) and its first position guess (the candidate) move with the subject.
  5. Angles. Exactly one of these sets pitch and yaw:
  6. reset: stored reset angles, or level pitch and the subject's heading;
  7. lock-on: aim at the lock target (below);
  8. front lock: turn smoothly to the subject's heading for up to a set time. It ends when the time runs out, when the angles come within a small tolerance, or on stick input. (The name is ours; we take it to be "reset the camera behind the character".)
  9. free: stick input times the rotation speed. Pitch stays between a minimum and a maximum. Pushing the stick hard up or down past a set angle ramps into a faster rotation that also moves pitch directly toward its limit.

Afterwards, unless the camera is locked on or in front lock, pitch drifts back toward a target pitch. 6. Placement and collision. It builds the look direction from the angles and keeps the fulcrum at its distance from the target. Sideways movement of the subject shortens that distance, between a minimum and a maximum rate. Then it avoids walls in one of two ways (see Keeping out of walls). 7. Chase. The final position eases toward the candidate. Up and down chase at one rate, the ground plane at another, and both depend on how far the direction has swung: below a minimum angle only the height follows, above a maximum angle both follow fully. Right after stick rotation the camera follows at once. 8. Orientation and checks. It builds the camera's axes and casts a sphere from the target to the final position. It clears the one-frame requests (reset, lock, boss lock, front lock). If any axis or the position is not a number, it restores the whole previous frame. Then it pushes the camera out of territories (below) and pulls it a safety margin toward the target.

Targeting and lock-on

While a lock is requested, the camera aims from its orbit point at a stored lock-target point. The pitch is raised by a shift that grows with the field of view and the row's lock-pitch-shift ratio, plus an angle from the distances involved. The pitch limits blend from the lock-on limits to the normal limits as the height difference to the target grows. The angles chase the goal at a lock rotation rate. Inside a dead angle the camera does not move. If lock adjustment is enabled, the stick nudges pitch and yaw up to a set maximum. The update does not choose the target: it only reads the point and the request flags.

Keeping out of walls

A global byte (0x05527a94, which we call the wall camera mode) picks one of two methods:

  • Zero: pull in. A sphere is cast from the target to the fulcrum, then to the candidate. On a hit the point moves to where the sphere stopped, but no closer than a minimum.
  • Non-zero: swing away. Two wing spheres are cast at a set angle to each side of the camera's line to the target. If exactly one hits, an escape yaw builds up and turns the camera away from that side. It builds at a strong rate while the hit is close, easing toward a weak rate as the hit lies farther along the probe. The swing is speed-limited, fades when nothing new is hit, and switches the chase rates to escape rates, which ease back afterwards. Stick input, lock-on, front lock and reset switch the swing off.

In both modes the final position gets one more sphere cast (step 8).

Territories are spheres in a list the update reaches through a global (0x0593e858). Each has a centre and a one-byte radius. If the camera is inside an enabled one, it moves back along its view axis to the sphere's surface, then a sphere cast checks the new position. A debug flag draws them.

Field of view

The vertical field of view (+0x50, radians) eases toward the parameter row's value in degrees. Below a minimum the row's value is replaced by a fixed minimum. Above a maximum it is capped at 48°. A flag raises the target point toward the height that half the view angle covers at the current distance, going halfway to it, so the subject stays in frame.

Which way the follow camera looks

The follow camera keeps its orientation as a direction it is looking along. Two small functions turn that into angles:

  • camera_follow_pitch (0x0183fad0) gives the up-down angle, from the direction's height against its length across the ground (an atan2), negated;
  • camera_follow_yaw (0x0183fb20) gives the left-right angle, from the direction's two ground components.

When the direction has no length to measure (both components zero), each falls back to the angle it stored last time instead of computing a meaningless one.

How these were verified
  • camera_update_follow: by @evill33t in #112, merged in #489 (commit 9786f4b9). 72 constructed edge cases pass against the original on stock code and under each BB_TARGET_FPS option (30, 60, uncapped) against the matching patch: 72/72 in all four runs. The cases cover both wall modes, the reset, lock and front-lock flags, chase limits, the safety margin, parameter selection with no subject, territories and their debug drawing, frame-time edge values, sphere-cast hits and misses, and callbacks that change the camera mid-update. An independent review rebuilt the change, reran every case and checked that deliberately planted mistakes are caught. It approved the change.
  • camera_follow_pitch and camera_follow_yaw: by @evill33t in #109, merged in #280. 32 and 30 edge cases pass, including not-a-number directions, and an independent review approved them.
  • All three are edge-verified only. No camera behaviour has been recorded in the running game.

Structs and globals

The follow camera (partial; the full size is unknown). The field names describe what the update does with each field; they are ours, not the game's.

Offset Fields
+0x10, +0x50, +0x60 camera transform (axes and position); vertical field of view (radians); subject transform
+0xa0–+0x110 points: target reference, target, cast origin, orbit origin, fulcrum, candidate, chased position; velocity
+0x13c–+0x15c reset requests; pitch and yaw (+0x140, read by the getters); target pitch; hand control
+0x180–+0x234 distances, sphere radius, safety margin; chase rates and angles (normal, escape, lock-on); pitch limits; rotation speeds
+0x240–+0x288 lock-on: rotation rate, dead angle, pitch shift, target point (+0x250), requests (+0x260–+0x262), front-lock timer and angles, stick adjustment
+0x28c–+0x2e0 wall escape (mode, yaw, rates, wing shape, hit fractions); territory flags and working transform
+0x320–+0x344 parameter IDs (override, event, boss lock, map) and chase rate; stick source; raise target with the field of view

Lock-camera parameter row: camera distance (+0x0), lowest pitch in degrees (+0x4), lock-pitch shift ratio (+0x8), height above the subject (+0xc), vertical field of view in degrees (+0x14). The table hangs off the solo parameter repository (0x05940340), at +0x9c0 (present when +0x9b8 is non-zero). The update reads three of its storage formats.

Globals: one-shot event and boss-lock IDs (0x05956678, +0x14 and +0x18), cleared every update; the wall camera mode (0x05527a94); the territory list (0x0593e858); the default stick vector (0x04b38000). The world sphere cast is 0x01c090e0.

Patch points

The frame-rate patches edit camera_update_follow. While our replacement is installed, its original bytes never run, so a patch line inside it does nothing. BB_TARGET_FPS rebuilds the same changes in code (see Frame timing):

Where Original 30 FPS++ / option 30 60 FPS++, Uncap FPS++ / options 60, uncapped
12 sites (0x0183ae75, 0x0183af8c, 0x0183ba97, 0x0183bc9b, 0x0183c30f, 0x0183c784, 0x0183cef0, 0x0183e2b2, 0x0183e7da, 0x0183e964, 0x0183f97e, 0x0183fa9f) fixed per-frame rates (parameters, distance, subject and target chase, lock and front-lock rotation, target-pitch drift, direct pitch, escape easing) unchanged each rate is rescaled from the measured frame time, capped at 1/60 s (1/30 s for parameter and escape easing)
0x0183dc96, 0x0183dcfe when both wings miss, the escape mode becomes 1; with the wall mode off, only the escape mode is cleared mode 0; with the wall mode off, the chase rates switch to the escape rates same
0x04d25e94 (constant) escape easing step 1/15 1/30
0x04d25e68 (constant) camera-distance easing rate 0.1 0.1

The two constants lie outside the function, so those patch lines still apply with the replacement installed: the option runs together with the patch, which supplies them, as it does for the other functions the option covers.

90 FPS++ and 120 FPS++ edit the same sites; we have no option for them.

Three other kinds of patch edit the function too, and also do nothing while it is replaced; none has a matching option. Disable Camera Auto Rotation via Movement blanks four instructions (0x0183c6e8, 0x0183c870, 0x0183c984, 0x0183dde6). Increased camera distance, Smaller Hunter and Smallest Hunter jump out at 0x0183ae68 (the last two also at 0x0183aeef); the first appears to scale the target camera distance by 1.5.

Open questions

  • Who calls camera_update_follow, and who sets the lock target, the lock and front-lock requests and the wall camera mode? Does the front lock really match the "camera behind the character" button? Is the wall mode a menu option?
  • The other fields of the parameter row (+0x10 is skipped) and the parameter file's identity.
  • What the carry-along pivot is (a moving platform or a mount, for example). Its velocity fields are set elsewhere.
  • Which of the 12 frame-rate sites carries which rate: the table matches them by count, not site by site. Which stage the auto-rotation patch's four sites belong to.
  • 0x0183a0e0, just before the update, is edited by the distance, Hunter-size and frame-rate patches. It is not mapped.
  • What the camera does in the running game: nothing has been recorded beyond constructed cases.
