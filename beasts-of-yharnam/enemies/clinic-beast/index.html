The Clinic Beast

The first enemy a new hunter meets: a beast feeding on a body in the hall below the operating room of Iosefka's Clinic. This page explains how it notices you and why it gets up, using Paleblood's decompiled code and our own recorded runs. Its attacks will be added once a fight has been recorded.

At a glance

First met
Iosefka's Clinic, in the hall below the operating room, where it feeds beside an arched door. Paleblood runs reach it from a save in the clinic by walking down the stairs.
AI code
The perception code every enemy runs: the sight test ai_sight_test at 0x016a52b0 and the perception update ai_perception_update at 0x016a85c0. Of the 15 functions on this page, 12 are verified and 3 are replaced and edge-tested at the revision below (sources).
Verified at
Paleblood 628534595723b2f61cea786f6c088f8e68139aa0: symbols/functions.csv, symbols/verification.csv and symbols/reviews.csv.
Still open
How it fights. Paleblood's renderer stops a few frames after the beast stands up, so no fight has been recorded yet.

Players' guides usually call it a scourge beast. This page calls it the clinic beast, because everything below was measured in this one encounter.

Frame by frame

The beast keeps feeding until the hunter is a few steps behind it. Then, within about two dozen frames, it turns its head and stands up. Each clip frame is one frame the renderer presented, and this run was slower than the real game, so read the order of events from the clip, not their duration. Keys: ← → step one frame, Space plays and pauses, L loops the current phase.

Timeline of the wake

The interactive timeline needs JavaScript. In words: feeding for frames 0 to 64, turning for 65 to 71, rising from 72 to the end.

The phase boundaries were read from the frames by eye. The clip shows what the beast does; the sections below explain why, as far as the code shows it.

How it notices you

In plain words

The beast has two senses that this page can explain: a nose and eyes. Hearing is in the same code but has not been decompiled yet.

  • Nose. Come close enough and it notices you, whichever way it is facing and whatever stands between you. verified
  • Eyes. Further away, it has to see you. You must be in a cone in front of it, neither too close nor too far, with nothing solid in between. How far it can see depends on how dark it is where you are standing. verified
  • What happened. In our runs the hunter always came from behind. The eyes never fired. The nose noticed the hunter at 3.5 metres. observed

Both rules are verified. Paleblood has replaced the sight test with readable C++, checked it against the original game code on 886 edge cases and 1,089 checks recorded from real runs, and an independent review session approved it (pull request #997).

The nose rule

The beast notices a character when the distance between them is shorter than its nose distance plus the sizes of both bodies. No angle is checked and no ray is cast, so walls do not block the nose. The nose distance is a number in each enemy's think data (its NpcThinkParam row). This wiki explains the rules and does not list the data values. verified

The sight test, step by step

If the nose has not noticed you, the eyes get their turn. Every step must pass: verified

  1. Reach, chosen by darkness. The enemy's data holds three eye distances: one for light, one for the dark and one for pitch dark. The test picks one by how dark it is around the target, multiplies it by the target's visibility and the enemy's own sight scale, then adds both bodies' sizes.
  2. Measured from behind the eyes. The distance is not measured from where the beast stands. It is measured from a point moved back along its facing, the eye back offset. So the tip of the cone sits behind the beast's body.
  3. Too close to see. Closer than the begin distance, measured from that point behind it, the eyes see nothing. At that range only the nose can notice you.
  4. Width. Lay the beast's facing and the line to you flat on the ground. The angle between them must be within the width angle. The data stores half the cone, so the full cone is twice that angle.
  5. Height. The line to you must not climb or drop more steeply than the height angle.
  6. Line of sight. When the caller asks for it, a ray is cast through the physics world from the beast's eye height to yours. If it hits anything, you are not seen. If no physics world is loaded, the ray step is skipped and you count as seen.

Then one override applies: a target marked unseeable is never noticed, whatever the test found. The test has three answers: not noticed, smelled (nose) or seen (eyes).

What happened in our runs

This comes from a recorded Paleblood run on 2026-11-13, in which every sight check the beast made was saved. observed

  • On the walk down, every check of the hunter came back not noticed, down to 6.84 m. The hunter was then about 120° away from the beast's facing, behind it, and out of reach of any forward eye cone.
  • The next check, at 3.48 m, came back smelled. By then the beast had already started to move off its spot. The checks after it show its facing swinging round towards the hunter.
  • In none of our runs did the beast's eyes see the hunter: every one approached from behind.
  • In an earlier run, watched frame by frame, it was still feeding with the hunter 4.0 m behind it and turned at about 3.6 m.

The checks are spaced out in time (see the target list), so this run only narrows the nose's reach down to somewhere between 3.5 and 6.8 m. Paleblood's replacement of the sight test gave the same answer as the original code on every recorded check. Those recordings became part of its verification in pull request #997.

The interactive range map needs JavaScript. In words: the hunter came from behind and to one side; the beast's checks found nothing at 6.84 m and smelled the hunter at 3.48 m.

Why it gets up

inferred Putting the run together with the code, the most likely chain is:

  1. its nose notices the hunter, and the hunter gets an entry on its target list;
  2. its alert level climbs to battle (the next section);
  3. the area's event logic, which has kept it in its feeding loop since the area loaded, sees that level and plays the rising animation. In our reading, being hit lets it go too.

The first step is code replaced in Paleblood plus recorded runs. The other two come from our static reading of code that is not in Paleblood source yet: the routine that raises an entry's level, and the event logic. Our runs do not fit a simpler trigger such as "the hunter entered the room". In the earlier run, the hunter stood well inside the room for many frames, and the beast reacted only at about 3.6 m.

Alert levels and the target list

Four levels

Each enemy carries a target state, which its scripts read to decide what to do. There are four levels. verified The script queries IsCautionState, IsFindState and IsBattleState test for levels 1, 2 and 3. GetPrevTargetState returns the level from the previous think. IsChangeState is true when the two differ.

The interactive state diagram needs JavaScript. In words: normal, caution, find and battle; a new target-list entry starts at caution or find; how an entry climbs to battle is not traced yet, apart from a "force battle" request.

The level is not stored and nudged up step by step. Each think, the perception update works it out again from the target list, in a fixed order of precedence. inferred Two special kinds of list entry mean battle. Failing those, the main target's own level counts. Failing that, two other special kinds mean caution. With nothing on the list, the level is normal. What the special kinds stand for is not known yet. The level it replaces becomes the previous level, so IsChangeState is true for exactly one think after a change. The perception update is replaced in Paleblood and tested on 802 edge cases.

The target list

The target list is what the beast has noticed: characters, and places it should go and look at.

  • Sensing about once a second. The list update senses the world about once a second, plus a random extra of up to a fifth of a second. The sensing of the many characters is spread out so they do not all sense on the same frame. inferred (static reading; Paleblood still runs this routine as original code)
  • Forgetting. Each entry has a clock. When it runs out, the entry's visible flag is cleared and the clock restarts from the enemy's sight forget time, a number in its data counted in 30ths of a second. Remembered places get a fixed 20 seconds. inferred (static reading)
  • Noticed again. When the sight test notices a character that is already on the list, its entry's clock is reset, it is marked visible, and it is flagged as noticed this think. Anything new gets a fresh entry at the front of the list. verified
  • The main target. The entry with the highest priority above −1 is the main target; among equals, the first one wins. Every other entry standing for the same thing is marked as matching it: the same character, or a place within half a metre. An entry that starts matching gets an eight-second recheck timer if its old one had run out. verified
  • "Can it see its target?" The script question IsVisibleTarget does not look for itself. It returns the visible flag that the sensing left on the current target. inferred (the lookup it calls is verified; the script binding itself is replaced and edge-tested)
  • Target speed. Every frame it records how fast its target is coming towards it, keeps the last 30 samples, and averages them with the newest weighted most. verified What uses this speed is not traced yet.

One piece of the machinery is already verified. The scripts' dice, GetRandam_Int, turns a 20-bit random fraction into a whole number from min to max, each about equally likely. verified Whether this beast's scripts use it to pick attacks is not shown yet.

How it actually works

The details

In the order the game runs them, once per think for each enemy:

  1. ai_perception_update (0x016a85c0) counts down a timer, runs the target-list update (0x016ab4c0, still original code), then sets the level and target type from the list.
  2. Inside the list update, a sight sweep builds one sight query per nearby character and runs ai_sight_test (0x016a52b0) on it. Each character noticed goes to ai_target_list_refresh_noticed (0x016b2760). ai_target_list_mark_main_matches (0x016b30a0) marks the entries that match the main target.
  3. The scripts read the result: IsBattleState and the other level queries, IsVisibleTarget (0x0165ca50, through ai_perception_target_visible at 0x016ad400) and the handle of the current target (ai_perception_current_handle, 0x016acfa0).
  4. Separately, every frame, ai_target_speed_record_update (0x0162d840) updates the speed record.

The AI system page describes the shared machinery these functions belong to.

Open questions

  • Hearing. The hearing test has been located (0x0169cef0 and 0x0169d160) but not decompiled. It would show whether the beast can hear you before it smells you.
  • How the level climbs. The routine that raises an entry from caution to find to battle (0x016b17c0) has not been read.
  • The wake. Confirming the chain in Why it gets up needs the event logic in Paleblood source, and a run that records the nose result and the event's reaction on the same frames.
  • Its attacks, which need the fight recording.
  • Its identity in the game's data. Which of the game's character types this beast is has not been established in Paleblood source yet.

Fighting it

These follow only from the rules above:

  • Sneaking up from behind only gets you so close. The nose works in every direction, so the beast notices you a few metres away whichever side you come from. In our runs it did so at about 3.5 m. See the nose rule.
  • Walls block its eyes, not its nose. A solid wall between you stops the line-of-sight ray, but the nose ignores walls. See the sight test.

What this page can and cannot claim

Every claim in this section is labelled by the kind of evidence behind it.

Label Meaning
observed Seen during play or in a recording, with its context: starting state, player position and actions, the visible change, and whether it repeats.
inferred A conclusion drawn from code or tests, stated with the scope that evidence covers.
unknown Not yet traced. It stays an open question, with the evidence needed next.
  • Observation is not implementation. An animation, health-bar change, phase transition, hit reaction, movement, or line of dialogue can be observed. On its own it does not identify an entity ID, function, data field, condition, timer, probability, script, or internal state.
  • No guessing. Nothing is filled in from genre convention, a gameplay impression, animation timing, or a displayed bar. Visually similar actions are not assumed to share code. No guessed names, IDs, catalogs, or numerical tables are published.
  • What a source claim needs. The entity, the function and address, the data or state it reads, the result it produces, public Paleblood source at the exact merged revision, tests, and independent review.
  • Evidence kinds differ. Recorded original-game behavior, static analysis, synthetic Paleblood tests, and verified replacements are different kinds of evidence. A link to one does not prove the others.
  • Tests have a scope. Passing tests support equivalence on the cases they cover, not untested gameplay situations. Timing and probability claims need measurements or directly verified logic, not a single encounter.
  • Shared systems are not specific actors. Code that every enemy runs does not, on its own, say what one enemy does. An enemy page ties shared code to one actor only through a recorded run of that encounter, and says so.
  • Absence is not a claim about the game. A missing profile describes the project's research, not what the game contains.

See Evidence & Media Review for how this wiki labels evidence.
