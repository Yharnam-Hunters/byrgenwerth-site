Weapons & Transformations

A transformed weapon is a visible change in how an equipped weapon looks and is used. That change is a starting point for research, not proof of the internal state layout, attack table, or transition function.

At a glance

What is verified
Nothing about weapon instances, transformation states, weapon-specific attack selection, or the functions that switch forms.
Easy to misread
The character update can fire W_ThrowAtk or W_ThrowDef throw-animation events. That is not transformation evidence.
Not published
No weapon catalog or numerical attack table without verified evidence.

The throw-animation event

The verified character update can fire a pending throw-animation event named W_ThrowAtk or W_ThrowDef. That is the only weapon-adjacent behavior in the current evidence, and it says nothing about weapon transformation logic. Its implementation and limits are described in Character and Stats & Progression.

What a source-backed profile needs

  1. Connect a weapon identifier to its state data and update path.
  2. Establish the transition conditions.
  3. Trace the animation/event handoff and attack selection.
  4. Measure any timing or resource effects.

Record observable transitions separately from source-derived explanations. If the code does not yet show whether a property is shared across weapons, leave that question open.

Related research: Combat & Damage, Movement & Stamina, and the Event system.

What this page can and cannot claim

Every claim in this section is labelled by the kind of evidence behind it.

Label Meaning
observed Seen during play or in a recording, with its context: starting state, player position and actions, the visible change, and whether it repeats.
inferred A conclusion drawn from code or tests, stated with the scope that evidence covers.
unknown Not yet traced. It stays an open question, with the evidence needed next.
  • Observation is not implementation. An animation, health-bar change, phase transition, hit reaction, movement, or line of dialogue can be observed. On its own it does not identify an entity ID, function, data field, condition, timer, probability, script, or internal state.
  • No guessing. Nothing is filled in from genre convention, a gameplay impression, animation timing, or a displayed bar. Visually similar actions are not assumed to share code. No guessed names, IDs, catalogs, or numerical tables are published.
  • What a source claim needs. The entity, the function and address, the data or state it reads, the result it produces, public Paleblood source at the exact merged revision, tests, and independent review.
  • Evidence kinds differ. Recorded original-game behavior, static analysis, synthetic Paleblood tests, and verified replacements are different kinds of evidence. A link to one does not prove the others.
  • Tests have a scope. Passing tests support equivalence on the cases they cover, not untested gameplay situations. Timing and probability claims need measurements or directly verified logic, not a single encounter.
  • Shared systems are not specific actors. The verified AI, Character, Event, and Physics findings do not yet identify any particular boss, enemy, NPC, weapon, or attack.
  • Absence is not a claim about the game. A missing profile describes the project's research, not what the game contains.

See Evidence & Media Review for how this wiki labels evidence.
